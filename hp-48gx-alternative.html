A replacement for the HP 48GX on a survey crew

Plenty of crews still carry an HP 48GX with survey programs loaded, because it works and nothing cheap replaced it. ChainLink Survey was built by a crew member in exactly that spot. He had the 48 in one pocket and a smartphone in the other, and no app that would record shots and run COGO on the phone.

What carries over

  • Keyed-in readings. You enter the angle and distance from the gun just like you do now, so it works with any total station.
  • DDD.MMSS angle entry. 90°15'30" is typed 90.1530, the same H.MMSS habit the 48 taught you.
  • The COGO you use: inverse, bearing and distance, intersections, resection, traverse closure, curves and areas.
  • Stakeout to points, lines and curves, with cut and fill in 3D.

What the calculator can't do

  • See the work. Every shot is drawn on a plot you can pan, zoom and tap to fix.
  • Keep every job. Each job has its own points, records and settings, stored on the phone with undo and redo.
  • Send it to the office. CSV, DXF, LandXML, KML and PDF, instead of copying numbers out of a field book.
  • Know where it is. One GPS fix puts the job on State Plane coordinates and draws it over aerial imagery, with your position shown.
  • Read the record. Paste a deed and it plots the calls, see the deed plotter.

Before you retire the 48

Bring your existing points along by exporting them as a coordinate list (point, northing, easting, elevation, description) and pasting it into ChainLink. The importer recognizes most layouts and lets you check everything before anything is added.

A phone's battery isn't a calculator's battery. Carry a small power bank, and export the job to CSV at the end of each day so you always have a copy off the phone.

Questions

What can replace an HP 48GX for surveying?

ChainLink Survey covers the field work a 48 with survey programs is used for: recording shots, COGO, traverse closure and stakeout, on the phone already in your pocket. It also stores jobs, draws the work on a plot and exports it to the office.

Does it use the same angle format as the HP 48?

Yes. Angles are entered as DDD.MMSS, so 90 degrees 15 minutes 30 seconds is typed 90.1530.
